The Drowned Hymn · Book One

A Man Turned the River

A kingdom built on a song. A boy who saw what the song hides.

In a world where nothing is written, a king is whatever the hymn says he is. Fifty years ago the river broke from its bed in the night and drowned a camp in the valley below. The hymn says the god did it, and on that hymn the king's line has ruled ever since.

But a herd-boy lay on the bank that night and saw a man with a pick axe.

The river did not rise. A man turned it.

The land of the story

Between the hills and the forest

The great river runs down from the mountain of the soma road, past the old clan's grassland on one bank and the meadows of the near hall on the other, and on into the forest.

The camp that drowned stood by the river, below the upper meadows.

The people of the story

The houses and the hearths

Every house in the land must acclaim the heir. These are the people you meet in Book One, grouped by where they live, with only what the first book tells you.

Portraits are imagined from the book; where the text is silent, the painter chose.
